MODEL TRANSPARENCY

From proposal to physics.

This is an engineering simulation of your proposed ESP32 / MPU6050 brushed quadcopter. It is not a measured digital twin, an ESP32 emulator, or a guarantee that the physical drone will fly.

What is physically simulated?

Bullet integrates a rigid body at 240 Hz with gravity and floor contact. Four rotor forces act at their arm positions; counter-rotating rotors generate yaw torque. Thrust saturation, 25 ms motor response, drag and external wind forces affect the flight.

What controls the drone?

A position PD loop and altitude PID loop set desired attitude and thrust. An attitude PD loop uses noisy orientation and body rates to allocate four motor forces. Hover, five beacons, gust recovery and link timeout use this same physics model.

How does this relate to your hardware?

The proposal’s ESP32, MPU6050, 8520 motors and 1S battery define the architecture. Mass (85 g), arm offsets (45 mm each axis), inertia and maximum thrust (0.45 N per motor) are assumptions. A real build needs measured thrust, weight, current, voltage sag and tuned firmware.

Important sensor boundary

The MPU6050 measures acceleration and rotation, not global position or height. Navigation and assisted landing here use virtual position/velocity feedback. Hardware needs additional sensing for these functions. The simulated Wi-Fi timeout is a logical experiment, not a network test. No GPS, computer vision or rescue detection is claimed.

Lab alignment

Experiment 1: 3D drone structure. Experiments 2–4: stability, hover and PID position control. Experiment 5: five beacons in a cross, using virtual local positioning rather than GPS. Experiments 6–7 are outside this implementation.
